Selqora: Echoes Beyond Earth

Stories of the machines NASA left behind on the Moon and Mars.

Live website: https://parvathykk2006.github.io/selqora/

Made by Parvathy KK (Space Apps team name: Selqora) for the 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge, responding to the challenge "Abandoned but not Forgotten: Storytelling about NASA's Discarded Equipment on the Moon and Mars".

Selqora is a student project made for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. It is not made or endorsed by NASA.

What it is

A child-friendly website (written for ages 8 to 12) where machines left on the Moon and Mars tell their own stories in the first person. Each story has a drawing, quick facts, a "Where am I now?" box and a link to the NASA page it came from.

The machines in this version
Machine	World	Mission
Eagle's landing stage and its science station (EASEP)	Moon	Apollo 11, 1969
Falcon's landing stage, and the separate ALSEP science station	Moon	Apollo 15, 1971
Sojourner, the first rover on Mars	Mars	Mars Pathfinder, 1997
How to use it
Look at the Moon map, or use the list of machines.
Tap a glowing dot or a name.
Read the story, then check the quick facts and the NASA link.
NASA sources

All facts come from these NASA pages and are paraphrased, not copied:

NASA NSSDCA, Apollo 11 Lunar Module / EASEP: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=1969-059C
NASA NSSDCA, Apollo 15 Lunar Module / ALSEP: https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=1971-063C
NASA Science, Mars Pathfinder: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-pathfinder/

Moon marker positions use NASA's landing coordinates: Apollo 11 at 0.674° N, 23.473° E and Apollo 15 at 26.132° N, 3.633° E.

How it is built
One file, index.html, using plain HTML, CSS, JavaScript and SVG.
No photos, no external images, fonts, scripts or libraries, and no network requests to other sites.
Hosted with GitHub Pages.
To run it on your own computer, download index.html and open it in a web browser.
AI assistance

The code, text and drawings were created with the help of an AI assistant (Claude, by Anthropic). The drawings carry an "AI-generated drawing" label on the website. The facts were checked against the NASA pages listed above. The slide deck for the challenge was also made with AI assistance.

Limitations
.The Moon map is a simplified illustration of the Moon's near side with a latitude and longitude grid. It is not a precise map.
.Mars has no map yet.
.Only three machines are included. More machines from NASA's resource list could be added later.
."Where am I now?" describes where NASA says each machine was left, not a live status.
